FX TREASURY PRODUCTS GUIDE

FORWARD CONTRACTS
A forward contract locks in an exchange rate for a future date.
Used when a corporate client has known future FX exposure.
Suitable for: importers, exporters, companies with foreign currency receivables or payables.
Tenor: typically 1 week to 12 months.
No upfront premium required.
Risk: client loses benefit if market moves favourably.

FX OPTIONS
An option gives the right but not the obligation to buy or sell currency at a specified rate.
Call option: right to buy foreign currency.
Put option: right to sell foreign currency.
Premium paid upfront by buyer.
Used when client wants downside protection while retaining upside.

CURRENCY SWAPS
Exchange of principal and interest in different currencies.
Used for longer-term hedging needs typically beyond 1 year.
Common in cross-border financing and liability management.

NON-DELIVERABLE FORWARDS (NDF)
Cash-settled forward for restricted currencies.
Settled in USD based on difference between agreed NDF rate and fixing rate.
Used for currencies with capital controls such as INR in offshore markets.

COLLAR STRATEGY
Combination of buying a put option and selling a call option.
Creates a range within which the exchange rate is protected.
Can be structured at zero net premium.
Limits both downside risk and upside participation.

PARTICIPATORY FORWARD
Client benefits partially if market moves favourably while retaining full downside protection.
Higher upfront premium than vanilla option.
Used when client wants protection plus some upside participation.

HEDGING POLICY GUIDELINES
Hedge ratio: typically 50-100% of known exposure.
Tenor match: hedge tenor should match underlying exposure.
Documentation: ISDA Master Agreement required for all derivative transactions.
Reporting: all hedges must be reported to treasury risk management.
Approval: large hedges require senior treasury approval.
Counterparty: only approved bank counterparties permitted.
