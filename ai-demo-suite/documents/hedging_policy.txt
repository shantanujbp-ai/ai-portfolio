CORPORATE TREASURY HEDGING POLICY GUIDELINES

APPROVED HEDGING INSTRUMENTS
1. FX Forward Contracts — for known future exposures up to 12 months
2. FX Options (vanilla) — when client requires upside participation
3. Currency Swaps — for exposures beyond 12 months
4. Collar Strategies — zero cost hedging within defined range
5. Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDF) — for restricted currencies
6. Participatory Forwards — partial upside with full downside protection

HEDGE RATIO GUIDELINES
Minimum hedge ratio: 50% of confirmed exposure
Maximum hedge ratio: 100% of confirmed exposure
Speculative positions: strictly prohibited for corporate clients
Documentation required: ISDA Master Agreement must be in place before any derivative transaction

APPROVAL LIMITS
Up to USD 5 million: Relationship Manager approval
USD 5 million to USD 25 million: Treasury Sales Head approval
Above USD 25 million: Head of Treasury approval
All structured products: Risk Committee approval regardless of size

ELIGIBLE COUNTERPARTIES
Only approved financial institutions permitted
Counterparty credit limit must be confirmed before execution
All counterparties must have signed ISDA Master Agreement
Annual counterparty review required

RISK MANAGEMENT REQUIREMENTS
Mark-to-market reporting: daily for all open positions
Breach reporting: immediate notification to Treasury Risk if limit exceeded
Hedge effectiveness testing: quarterly for all designated hedges
Rollover policy: forward contracts may be rolled with approval, maximum 3 times

DOCUMENTATION REQUIREMENTS
Trade confirmation: within 24 hours of execution
ISDA documentation: must be fully executed before first trade
Board reporting: quarterly summary of hedging activity
Audit trail: all hedge decisions must be documented with business rationale

AI AND TECHNOLOGY GOVERNANCE FOR TREASURY
GenAI systems used for treasury advisory must be grounded in approved product documentation.
AI-generated hedging recommendations require human review before client communication.
All AI model outputs must be traceable to source documents via retrieval-augmented generation.
Hallucination risk must be mitigated through mandatory grounding checks on all AI outputs.
AI systems must not autonomously execute transactions without explicit human approval.
