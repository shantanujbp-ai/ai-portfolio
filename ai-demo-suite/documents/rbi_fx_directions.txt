RBI MASTER DIRECTION - RISK MANAGEMENT AND INTER-BANK DEALINGS
FX HEDGING FRAMEWORK - SUMMARY
Source: RBI Circular RBI/2023-24/108, A.P. (DIR Series) Circular No. 13, January 5, 2024
Effective: April 5, 2024

PURPOSE
This circular establishes the regulatory framework for hedging foreign exchange risks
in India. It consolidates all directions for foreign exchange transactions including
cash, tom, spot, and derivative transactions.

USER CLASSIFICATION
Authorised dealers must classify users into retail and non-retail categories.
Non-retail users: entities with minimum net worth of INR 500 crore, or
central government, state governments, residents with foreign currency borrowings
above USD 10 million, and listed companies.
Retail users: all entities that do not qualify as non-retail.

PERMITTED INSTRUMENTS FOR RETAIL USERS
Foreign exchange cash, tom, and spot transactions.
Forward contracts up to 12 months without underlying documentation for hedging
contracted exposures up to USD 10 million per financial year.
Plain vanilla options.
Cost reduction structures are not permitted for retail users.

PERMITTED INSTRUMENTS FOR NON-RETAIL USERS
All instruments permitted for retail users.
Foreign exchange swaps.
Cross currency swaps.
Interest rate swaps.
Cost reduction structures permitted subject to conditions.
Structured products with prior approval.

DOCUMENTATION REQUIREMENTS BEFORE EXECUTING DERIVATIVE TRANSACTIONS
Before executing any foreign exchange derivative transaction, authorised dealers must obtain:
1. User Declaration: Written declaration from the client stating the nature and purpose of the derivative transaction.
2. Exposure Confirmation: Written confirmation that the underlying exposure is genuine and not artificially created for speculation.
3. Threshold Documentation: For exposures above prescribed thresholds, supporting documents evidencing the underlying exposure must be submitted to the authorised dealer before execution.
4. Suitability Assessment: For retail users, the authorised dealer must conduct and document a suitability assessment before offering derivative products.
5. Appropriateness Assessment: For all users, an appropriateness assessment must be conducted and documented.
6. ISDA Agreement: For OTC derivatives, an ISDA Master Agreement or equivalent documentation must be in place before first trade execution.
7. Board Policy Confirmation: The authorised dealer must confirm the transaction falls within its board-approved derivatives policy.

RISK MANAGEMENT REQUIREMENTS
Authorised dealers must have board-approved policy for derivative transactions.
Suitability assessment must be conducted for retail users.
Appropriateness assessment must be conducted for all users.
Mark-to-market reporting must be provided to clients at regular intervals.
Dealers must ensure derivatives are used exclusively for hedging, not speculation.

KEY CHANGES FROM PRIOR FRAMEWORK
Simplified documentation: users can hedge up to USD 10 million without submission
of underlying documents, subject to self-declaration.
Dynamic hedging: users may cancel and rebook forward contracts up to limits
specified by RBI without restriction.
Net hedging: non-retail users may hedge net exposure across multiple underlying
transactions.
Centralised hedging: multinational companies may hedge exposures of overseas
group entities through Indian authorised dealer.

ENFORCEMENT
Violations attract penalties under Foreign Exchange Management Act 1999.
Authorised dealers are responsible for ensuring compliance by their clients.
RBI may cancel authorised dealer licence for systemic violations.
